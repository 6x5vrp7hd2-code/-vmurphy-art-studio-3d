Questions

Originals, editions and usage.

A straightforward guide to what is original, what is newly created, how reproductions work and how digital files may be used.

What counts as an original V. Murphy work?

Only paintings actually created by V. Murphy belong to the Original Collection. New digitally developed pieces are kept in the separate Studio Editions collection.

Does V. Murphy still paint?

No. The original archive is finite and will grow online only as surviving works are catalogued.

Are all catalogue titles original?

No. Where an original title is unknown, the family uses a descriptive working title rather than inventing a history around the piece.

How are Studio Editions created?

Customers choose from structured options such as setting, architecture, palette, mood, features and format. The studio uses that brief to prepare candidate digital compositions.

Can my Studio Edition remain private?

A private-edition option can keep the finished piece out of the standard catalogue, subject to terms explaining the limits of absolute uniqueness in digital-generation workflows.

Can I print a digital download myself?

Yes, subject to the personal-use licence attached to the product.

Can I use a download for invitations or menus?

Approved personal uses can include invitations, menus, place cards and personal stationery. Business use requires an appropriate licence.

Can businesses licence an image?

Yes. Licensing can cover hospitality, events, publishing, packaging, advertising or other agreed uses.
